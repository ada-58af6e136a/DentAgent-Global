MATERIAL ENTRIES

Wieland zirconia (full-ceramic, layered): Wieland zirconia is a high-strength ceramic material known for excellent aesthetics and good biocompatibility. It is recommended for anterior and premolar restorations where natural appearance is the priority. The material has a flexural strength of approximately 900 MPa, making it suitable for single crowns and short-span bridges up to three units. It is not recommended for patients with severe bruxism or for long-span posterior bridges under heavy occlusal load — consider monolithic zirconia or PFM in those cases. Wieland zirconia is available in standard and high-translucency grades; the high-translucency grade is preferred for anterior upper restorations where light transmission matters most. It is metal-free, which eliminates the risk of dark metal margins showing at the gumline over time.

IPS e.max (lithium disilicate, full-ceramic): IPS e.max is a lithium disilicate glass-ceramic produced by Ivoclar Vivadent. It offers the highest level of translucency and natural light transmission of any material we work with, making it the preferred choice for upper anterior restorations where mimicking natural tooth appearance is critical. Its flexural strength is approximately 400 MPa, which is lower than zirconia — it is therefore not suitable for posterior full-arch cases, patients with bruxism, or bridges longer than three units. e.max crowns can be bonded adhesively, which allows for more conservative tooth preparation than PFM. We offer e.max in both pressed and CAD-milled versions; CAD-milled is standard for single crowns and short spans.

Monolithic zirconia (high-strength, full-contour): Monolithic zirconia is a single-block, full-contour zirconia restoration with no layered porcelain. Its flexural strength ranges from 1,000 to 1,200 MPa, making it the strongest material we offer and the recommended choice for posterior restorations in patients with bruxism, heavy bite forces, or a history of chipping porcelain restorations. Because it is milled from a single zirconia block without a porcelain overlay, the risk of chipping is effectively eliminated. Aesthetics are acceptable for posterior teeth but are not comparable to layered zirconia or e.max for anterior use — the material has lower translucency and a more opaque appearance. It is available in standard white and multi-layered gradient shades for improved colour blending.

PFM crown (porcelain-fused-to-metal, Vita VM9 porcelain over cobalt-chrome alloy): PFM crowns consist of a cast metal substructure with porcelain fired on top. They have a long clinical track record and remain a reliable, cost-effective choice for posterior restorations where strength is prioritised over maximum aesthetics. The metal substructure provides excellent marginal fit and durability. The main aesthetic limitation is the potential for a dark metal margin line to become visible at the gumline if gum recession occurs over time. PFM is suitable for patients with heavy bite forces, long-span bridges, and cases where the budget does not support full-ceramic alternatives. We use Vita VM9 porcelain over a cobalt-chrome alloy base as standard. Precious metal alloy (gold-based) substructures are available on request at additional cost.

PMMA temporary crown (polymethyl methacrylate, provisional): PMMA is used for provisional or temporary restorations while a permanent restoration is being fabricated or during an implant healing period. It is not intended as a long-term solution. PMMA provisionals are lightweight, easy to adjust, and tooth-coloured. They have low fracture resistance compared to ceramic or zirconia and should not be used in high-load posterior positions for extended periods. Standard lead time for a PMMA provisional is 2 to 3 business days. We recommend PMMA for implant cases requiring an extended osseointegration period of 3 months or more, or for cases where the final shade or form needs to be evaluated by the patient before the permanent restoration is confirmed.

Valplast flexible partial denture (thermoplastic nylon resin): Valplast is a metal-free thermoplastic material used for removable partial dentures. Its clasps are made from the same flesh-coloured nylon material as the denture base, making them nearly invisible when in place — this is its primary advantage over conventional metal-clasp partial dentures. Valplast is recommended for patients who cannot receive a fixed restoration and who have an aesthetic concern about visible metal clasps. It is not suitable as a long-term replacement for extensive posterior support or cases requiring rigid cross-arch stability. Valplast partials cannot be easily relined or repaired in the same way as acrylic partials, and additions are not possible after fabrication. It is best suited for bounded saddle cases with good remaining dentition.

MATERIAL COMPARISONS

Zirconia vs. PFM (porcelain-fused-to-metal): Zirconia crowns offer better aesthetics, no dark metal margin line, and are entirely metal-free — making them preferable for anterior teeth and patients with metal sensitivities. PFM crowns are more cost-effective, have a longer clinical track record, and remain a reliable choice for posterior teeth in patients with heavy bite forces or tight budgets. For anterior teeth where aesthetics are the primary concern, zirconia is generally preferred. For posterior teeth in patients with bruxism or where cost is a significant factor, PFM remains a sound clinical choice.

IPS e.max vs. zirconia (layered): IPS e.max produces a more lifelike, translucent result than zirconia for anterior restorations because its optical properties more closely mimic natural enamel. However, e.max has lower fracture resistance (approximately 400 MPa vs. 900 MPa for layered zirconia) and is not suitable for high-load posterior positions or bruxism cases. For a patient requiring a single upper anterior crown where the highest aesthetic outcome is the priority, e.max is the better choice. For premolars, posterior teeth, or patients with parafunctional habits, zirconia is the safer option.

Monolithic zirconia vs. layered zirconia: Monolithic zirconia is a single milled block with no porcelain overlay, giving it a flexural strength of 1,000–1,200 MPa and zero chipping risk. Layered zirconia has porcelain fired over a zirconia core, producing a more natural appearance — but the porcelain layer carries a small risk of chipping under heavy load. For posterior teeth, especially in bruxism patients or cases with limited interocclusal space, monolithic zirconia is preferred. For anterior teeth where maximum aesthetics are required, layered zirconia (or e.max) is the better choice despite the slightly higher chipping risk.

Zirconia vs. e.max for a single upper central incisor: For a single upper central incisor where the patient has a high aesthetic demand and a natural adjacent tooth to match, IPS e.max is generally the first choice due to its superior translucency and light-scattering properties. High-translucency layered zirconia is an acceptable alternative if the patient has a history of chipping or if the dentist prefers a stronger material. Standard (non-high-translucency) zirconia is not recommended for upper central incisors in aesthetically demanding cases due to its more opaque appearance.

PFM vs. monolithic zirconia for a posterior molar in a bruxism patient: For a posterior molar in a patient with confirmed bruxism, monolithic zirconia is the preferred choice. It has higher strength than PFM and eliminates the risk of porcelain chipping entirely. PFM remains acceptable if the patient has budget constraints, but the porcelain layer in a PFM crown can chip under repeated heavy occlusal loading in bruxism patients, requiring repair or replacement. Monolithic zirconia is the more durable long-term solution for this scenario.
