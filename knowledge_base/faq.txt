Q: What material do you recommend for a patient with bruxism?
A: For patients with bruxism, we strongly recommend monolithic zirconia. It has a flexural strength of 1,000 to 1,200 MPa, making it highly resistant to fracture under heavy occlusal load. We do not recommend layered ceramic, IPS e.max, or feldspathic porcelain for bruxism cases, as these materials are significantly more prone to chipping under repeated high-force contact. If aesthetics matter for anterior teeth, high-translucency monolithic zirconia offers a good compromise between strength and appearance.

Q: What is the difference between monolithic zirconia and layered zirconia?
A: Monolithic zirconia is milled from a single block with no separate porcelain layer. It is extremely strong at 1,000 to 1,200 MPa, resistant to chipping, and the most cost-effective zirconia option — best suited to posterior teeth. Layered zirconia uses a zirconia coping with hand-applied porcelain on the visible surfaces, offering superior aesthetics and better light transmission for anterior restorations, but at a higher price and with a slightly higher chipping risk under heavy bite forces.

Q: What is the difference between zirconia and PFM?
A: Zirconia crowns are completely metal-free, have no dark metal margin line at the gumline, offer better long-term biocompatibility, and look more natural under various lighting conditions. PFM crowns have a cobalt-chromium substructure that can show a grey margin over time if gum recession occurs, but they are more cost-effective and have a very long clinical track record. For anterior teeth or patients with metal sensitivity, zirconia is generally preferred. For posterior teeth with heavy bite forces or significant budget constraints, PFM remains a reliable option.

Q: What is IPS e.max and when should I use it?
A: IPS e.max is a lithium disilicate ceramic produced by Ivoclar Vivadent, known for exceptional translucency and a natural appearance that closely mimics natural tooth structure. Its flexural strength is approximately 360 to 400 MPa. IPS e.max is ideal for anterior crowns, veneers, inlays, and onlays where appearance is the primary clinical priority. It is not recommended for patients with bruxism or for high-load posterior restorations. Both pressed and CAD-milled versions are available — pressed generally delivers slightly better aesthetics for veneers and complex anterior cases.

Q: What material do you recommend for anterior teeth where aesthetics are the priority?
A: For anterior teeth where aesthetics are the primary concern, we recommend either high-translucency layered Wieland Zenostar zirconia or IPS e.max lithium disilicate. High-translucency Wieland Zenostar zirconia offers better durability, while IPS e.max offers the most natural light transmission and is preferred for patients with the highest aesthetic demands. For veneers specifically, pressed IPS e.max is typically our first recommendation.

Q: What material do you recommend for posterior teeth?
A: For posterior teeth, we recommend monolithic zirconia as the first choice. It offers the best balance of strength, durability, and cost for molar and premolar restorations. If aesthetics in the premolar region are important and the patient is not a bruxer, high-translucency monolithic zirconia is a good option. PFM is a reliable and cost-effective alternative for posterior teeth in patients with heavy bite forces or budget constraints.

Q: What is PEEK and when is it used?
A: PEEK (polyether ether ketone) is a high-performance thermoplastic polymer used for implant-supported frameworks, full-arch implant bridges, and some removable prosthetic superstructures. It is completely metal-free, very lightweight, and has excellent biocompatibility. PEEK is used as an alternative to titanium or zirconia for implant frameworks in patients with confirmed metal sensitivities, and for full-arch implant restorations where weight reduction is a clinical priority. Please contact us to discuss case suitability before ordering.

Q: What are the production stages my order goes through?
A: Your case passes through these stages in sequence: (1) Impression receipt — case details are recorded and the order is checked for completeness. (2) Inspection — the technical team confirms the impression or scan is usable. (3) CAD design — technicians design the restoration digitally. (4) Milling or pressing — the restoration is fabricated from the selected material. (5) Porcelain application — for layered restorations, ceramic is applied and fired in multiple stages. (6) Finishing and polishing — the restoration is refined and adjusted for occlusion. (7) Quality control — a senior technician inspects the final result against the prescription. (8) Dispatch — the case is packaged and shipped with a tracking number.
